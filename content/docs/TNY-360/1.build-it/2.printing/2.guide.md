# Printing Guide

Let's print this TNY-360!

In this guide, you'll see how to 3D print the parts of the TNY-360 with the right settings, materials, and orientations to ensure optimal performance and durability.

---

## Material Selection

Choosing the right filament material is crucial for the robot's structural integrity and thermal safety.  
Here are the recommended materials for each part of the robot, along with the reasons for these choices:

| **Part**             | **Recommended Material** | **Reason**                                                                            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Structural Parts** | PETG                     | Offers a good balance of strength, heat resistance & durability.                      |
| **Robot Feet/Paws**  | TPU 95A                  | Provides shock absorption, and deformation for the contact sensor to work.            |
| **Aesthetic Covers** | PLA or other             | Suitable for non-structural parts, allowing for easier printing & more color options. |

::info-box{title="Using other materials" type="warning"}
While using other materials like PLA or ABS may work, we cannot guarantee the same performance, durability, or behavior as the recommended materials.  
The robot has been designed and tested with PETG printed parts, and using other materials may lead to deformation, breakage, or a different weight distribution that could affect the robot's functionality.
::

---

## Print Settings

The TNY-360 is designed to be printed with a standard FDM 3D printer. However, to ensure the best results, we recommend using the following settings in your slicer:


| **Parameter**       | **Recommended Value** | **Reason**                                                                                                       |
| ------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Infill Pattern**  | Gyroid                | Spreads forces evenly across the part, providing better strength and durability while keeping weight low.        |
| **Infill Density**  | 15%                   | Provides sufficient strength for structural components while minimizing weight, crucial for optimal performance. |
| **Number of walls** | Minimum of 3          | Ensures the outer shell is strong enough to withstand mechanical stresses and impacts.                           |

::info-box{title="Aesthetic Covers" type="info"}
If you want to save time or filament, you can print the aesthetic covers with a lower infill density (e.g., 10%) and less walls (e.g., 2 walls). These parts are not structural and do not require the same level of strength as the other components.
::

::info-box{title="The slower, the better!" type="warning"}
As we will see in the next section, the TNY-360 parts are designed to be printed without supports.  
However, to ensure the best quality and strength of the parts, we **strongly recommend printing at a slower speed** than what your printer is capable of.

These days, most FDM printers can print at insane speeds, but this often comes at the cost of quality and strength *(especially for PETG)*.
We recommend printing parts with slight overhangs and holes at a speed of **100 mm/s or lower**.
::

## Part Names, Orientation & Supports

### Naming Convention

All the STL files of the TNY-360 parts follow a specific naming convention to help you identify them easily. These naming conventions are commonly used in the 3D printing community, so you should feel at home if you already have experience with that!

Here's a breakdown of the naming convention used for the TNY-360 parts:

- **Color**: `[color]`  
    The color of the part, e.g., `[b]` for black, `[w]` for white.  
    *For some special parts, you may see `[calibration]` or `[tpu]` instead. This indicates the material or purpose of the part (calibration part, or flexible TPU part).*
- **Part Name**: `part_name`  
    The name of the part, e.g., `torso_top`, `head_backplate`, etc.  
    This is the main identifier for the part and describes its function or location in the robot.
- **Quantity**: `xN`  
    The quantity of the part in the robot, e.g., `x1`, `x2`, etc.  
    This indicates how many of this part are needed for the complete assembly of the robot.

::info-box{title="Examples" type="note"}
`[b]-torso_top_x1` => Top part of the Torso, black color, 1 piece needed.  
`[w]-leg_bottom_cover_base_L_x2.stl` => Base Cover for the Leg Bottom, white color, 2 pieces needed.  
`[tpu]-paw_x4` => Paw part, TPU material (95A), 4 pieces needed.
::

### Print Orientation

Part orientation is an important factor in 3D printing, as it can affect the strength, surface finish, and print time of the parts.

This is why, for every part of the TNY-360, **all the STL files have been oriented in the recommended position for printing**. Just drag and drop the files into your slicer, and they will be ready to print.

All parts have been designed to be printed **without supports**, so you can print them as-is without any additional settings, although you may want to add supports for some sections of the parts (holes and overhangs) if you want to ensure a cleaner finish.
Depending on your print bed, **you may also want to add a brim or raft on some bigger parts** *(like the Torso-Bottom and Torso-Top parts)* to avoid any warping.

### The `TNY-360.3mf` file

Alternatively, you can download the `TNY-360.3mf` file, which contains all the parts of the robot in a single file, already oriented and with the recommended settings for printing.

Use this file as a starting point for your prints, and feel free to adjust the settings based on your specific printer and filament to achieve the best results *(adding supports, changing the print settings, etc.)*.
