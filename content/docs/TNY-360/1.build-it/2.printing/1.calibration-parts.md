# Calibration Parts

The TNY-360 can be fully 3D-printed with a standard FDM 3D printer.

However, since this robot needs precise tolerances for screws and components, we'll make sure your printer is perfectly calibrated before you start printing the parts!

---

## Check your printer settings

The TNY-360 is pretty forgiving when it comes to clearances and tolerances, but it's still a good thing to check your printer settings before you start printing the parts.

To ensure perfect dimensions and print quality, we recommend checking the following settings in your slicer:

- **Hole compensation**:  
    Double-check your slicer settings for hole compensation. Adjust the hole compensation value to ensure that holes are printed with the correct diameter.
- **Elephant foot compensation**:  
    Make sure your slicer is set to compensate for elephant foot (the slight plastic bulge at the bottom of prints). This will help ensure that the parts with clips fit together properly.

If you want to make sure your printer is perfectly calibrated, you can look at the fantastic [OrcaSlicer Calibration Guide](https://www.orcaslicer.com/wiki/#calibrations) to calibrate your printer and filament.

---

## Print the calibration parts

If you printer is well calibrated, you only have to print two calibration parts to make sure your heat inserts and servomotor screws fit perfectly in the printed parts :

::split{side="right"}
#left
### 1 - Heat Insert Calibration Part
The Heat Insert Calibration Part is used to check the fit of the heat inserts in the printed parts in all directions.

### 2 - Servomotor Screw Calibration Part
The Servomotor Screw Calibration Part is used to check the fit of the servomotor screws in the printed parts.

#right
![Calibration Parts](/docs/images/V2/Printing/Calibration/CalibrationParts.jpg){width=100%}
::

::info-box{title="What if the Heat Inserts or Screws don't fit?" type="warning"}
If they don't fit in the printed parts, you have two options :
- **1 - Adjust the hole compensation** in your slicer and reprint the parts *(the easier option)*.
- **2 - Download the FreeCAD files**, increase the Heat Insert holes diameter in the `Settings.FCStd` file, and re-export the STL files to print the parts again *(more complex)*.

Also, **If the vertical holes don't seem round**, you can adjust the **Side Hole Compensation** parameter in the `Settings.FCStd` file to make the holes rounder.
::
